**(a) What is it analyzing?** A post-mortem of an AI screening experiment: someone fed a Claude model the GAAP XBRL financial statements of companies that later got hit with SEC enforcement (AAERs) or restatements, plus a control group of companies with no enforcement, and asked it to score misstatement risk. This document dissects every error the model made — 5 false positives out of 23 controls, 2 misses out of 9 real cases, and three "holdout" cases (Hub Group, Weis Markets, Genie) chosen so the model couldn't have memorized the outcome — sorting each error into: made something up, misread real numbers, bad answer key, or the signal wasn't in the data at all.

**(b) Most important honest admission?** The HUBG reframing in §3. They flagged Hub Group before/around its 2026 restatement — the headline "we caught one" — and then deliberately deflate it: the model flagged HUBG for its *2018* amendment history and goodwill growth, not the actual purchased-transportation/AP understatement. "Tier hit / mechanism miss." That's exactly the admission a vendor pitching this tool would bury. Runner-up: the calibration didn't improve between waves (§4), and they say so flatly.

**(c) One error case and what it teaches.** WMK (Weis Markets): ~$22M of inventory overstatement accumulated over four years at a single meat plant. The model went digging in receivables/DSO and missed it — but the deeper point is that $22M spread over four years in a grocery chain's consolidated inventory is below the noise floor of ratio analysis. Any analyst knows this instinctively: consolidated statements launder plant-level problems. The lesson is that materiality-at-the-segment-level frauds are invisible to top-of-house screens, human or machine. (BRX teaches the sibling lesson: manipulation of a non-GAAP metric like same-property NOI never touches the GAAP lines the model reads.)
