(a) What it's claiming, in my words: Someone built a backtest asking whether an LLM, given only pre-cutoff filings, could have flagged companies that later became SEC enforcement cases. They say yes, statistically (AUC 0.83, p≈0.001 vs matched controls) — but then voluntarily undercut their own headline twice: part of the signal was the model recognizing famous company names rather than reading the accounting (masking identities moved Hertz down 23 points and Monsanto up 30), and there's no threshold where it works as an actual screen (catch-all setting = 71% false alarms; low-false-alarm setting catches 1 of 12). Pitch is "the caveats are the product, come audit my repo."

(b) Does anything smell overstated vs the numbers given? The framing is unusually honest, but a few things:

- **AUC 0.83 [0.62, 0.98]** — that lower bound is close to a coin flip. The post does flag "small N, wide interval," but leading with 0.83 while the CI admits "possibly barely better than random" is the classic move. With 12 cases, this is anecdote-adjacent.
- **The contamination measurement is the real tell**: name-identification only dropped from 50% to ~22% under masking, meaning the model still identifies the company a fifth of the time. So even the "masked" numbers aren't clean. The post presents measuring contamination as a virtue — fair — but doesn't say what AUC survives after masking, which is the only number I'd actually care about. Conspicuous omission.
- **Hindsight-selection risk**: the 12 cases became enforcement actions, so they were selected on an outcome correlated with size, press coverage, and restatement drama — exactly the things "industry/size/era-matched" controls may not fully match. Not addressed in 60 seconds of reading.
- Nothing is fabricated-smelling; the internal numbers are consistent. It's overstated only in ordering — the honest headline is "no usable signal after decontamination, here's why," and the post buries that as finding #3.

(c) Would I click through? Yes, but not for alpha — this is explicitly a negative-ish result and useless as a screen by its own admission (12/12 detection at 71% false positives is worthless in a live book). I'd click for two reasons: the memorization-contamination measurement is directly relevant to anyone tempted to bolt an LLM onto a fundamental screen (vendors pitching this never show that number), and "one command, no accounts, fully reproducible" is rare enough that I'd want to see if it holds. The self-flagellating framing also reads as credible rather than promotional, which ironically is what earns the click. What would stop me: if the README leads with the 0.83 and I have to dig for the masked-performance number, I'd close the tab and assume the honesty was marketing.
