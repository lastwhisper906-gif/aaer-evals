# Gap list, 2026-11-08: where the automated analysis differs from how practitioners work

Source of every practice statement below: `reports/헤지펀드 회계 재무 분석 실무.md` (the research report) and the five notes under `research_notes/헤지펀드 회계 재무 분석 실무/`. Every quotation in double quotes is copied from one of those six files character for character, with the URL the report gives for it; where a URL's path carries a capital-and-digit segment the plain-name check reads as a code, one character is percent-encoded, which resolves to the same address. Words of the repository's own files are set in italics with their file and line, never in double quotes. What the repository does today is cited to a file and line. Nothing here is a finding about a company.

Row shapes. A **task row** is written exactly as `docs/next_cycle_tasks.md` takes it: `[ ] title · builds · judge · expected value from · PR`, with the judge a command and the expected value from a filing, from companyfacts through `tests/companyfacts_source.py`, from a paper's worked example, or planted by the test, never from a run of the code it judges. ## B. Gaps, in order of practitioner weight

### B.1 Reading order and day-one cross-checks

**What practitioners do.** The cash-flow working-capital lines are re-derived from the balance-sheet deltas and the residual is attributed to a short list of causes, with the remainder left visible: "Every working capital line agrees to the balance sheet movement for that account, or carries a named non-cash adjustment explaining the difference" and otherwise "UNEXPLAINED — DO NOT PLUG" (https://github.com/adoptai/cpa-skills/blob/dev/markdown/cash-flow-tieout.md). Mulford names the acquisition path: "No disclosures are provided of the extent to which acquisitions have boosted operating cash flow." (https://nacm.org/pdfs/gscfm/6.%46W6._Creative_Cash_Flow.pdf). Schilit reads the three sections against each other: "you move all the good stuff into operating cash flow, and anything that is going to be a minus to cash flow you move into the second or third section (investing or financing)." (https://irmagazine.com/reporting/shenanigans-qa).

**What the repository does today.** `articulation_gap` is printed as one number per account (`src/calculator.py:1119-1122`) with a note that an acquisition or translation is an honest cause (`src/articulation.py:1-10`); nothing subtracts the acquired balances the business-combination note now hands the analyst as `note_tables` facts (`docs/next_cycle_tasks.md:137`; Littelfuse's acquired receivables in `queue.md:101`), nor write-offs, nor the translation line. A new cash-flow caption is a model key only (`docs/HEDGE_FUND_ANALYSIS.md:244`, `cash_flow_classification_shift`); caption footing is §5.1 item 8 (`:497`).

**Delta.** The gap is reported but never decomposed, so the analyst cannot tell an acquisition's receivables from a receivable sale, and the research says the residual after the knowable causes is the flag.

### B.2 Ratio construction

**What practitioners do.** Forensic days are ending balance over the current period's flow. Schilit: "For financial shenanigan detection purposes, we recommend calculating DSI using the ending balance of inventory (rather than the average balance, as some companies and texts may advise)." (https://read-online-books.com/book/read-online-financial-shenanigans-how-to-detect-accounting-gimmicks-fraud-in-financial-reports-3rd-edition-free/page-21). Kerrisdale's footnote: "Days sales outstanding calculated as most recent quarter-end receivables / quarterly revenue * (365/4), and is not average DSO." (https://www.kerrisdalecap.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/%433.ai-Auditor-Note.pdf). SEC staff use the same convention: "your day's sales outstanding, calculated based on the most recent quarter's sales, increased from 37.0 days at both December 31, 2012 and 2011 to 49.2 days at June 30, 2013." (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1232524/000000000013057994/filename1.pdf). Sold receivables are added back: "When we include factored receivables that COTY removes from the balance sheet, we see that payment cycles have extended by 19 days over the last two years" (https://peekbehindthenumbers.substack.com/p/accounting-and-earnings-quality-signals); Schilit: "Be sure to adjust for these moves in calculating Days in Receivable or other related metrics." (https://www.scribd.com/document/465460323/Financial-Shenanigans). Each day is translated into revenue: "Each day of receivables represents approximately $123 million in sales." (same BTN post). Growth is tested against expected growth, not only last year's: the Coldwater Creek test in the report compares inventory growth with management's expected revenue growth. Supplier finance beyond normal terms is debt: "S&P Global Ratings, however, treats trade payables that remain outstanding after 90 days under a supplier finance arrangement as a form of borrowing." (https://www.gtreview.com/news/americas/moodys-warns-over-unreasonable-supply-chain-finance-payment-terms/).

**What the repository does today.** The trend table computes days on the ending balance over the period's own flow (`src/trends.py:273`, `:278`, `receivables / revenue * days_in_period`), which is the practitioner convention. The calculator's `ratios.efficiency` is the other one: `average receivables / revenue * 365` over trailing four quarters (`src/calculator.py:972-983`, averages at `:913`, `DAYS_IN_YEAR` at `:120`), and that is the field both analysts are told to cite (`financial-analyst.md:53-55`; `accounting-analyst.md:70-71`). Nothing adds sold receivables back into any days measure; `proceeds_from_sale_of_receivables` is a flow, not the period-end balance sold (`src/trends.py:191`). The supplier-finance obligation is a balance (`terms.balances_now.supplier_finance_obligation`) that no days or leverage ratio reclassifies; the rollforward tags and `operating_cash_flow_without_supplier_finance_or_receivables_sold` are §5.2 item 23 (`docs/HEDGE_FUND_ANALYSIS.md:519`). Growth against guided growth needs the guidance ledger, §5.2 item 33 (`:529`).

**Delta.** The two conventions coexist and the analysts are pointed at the one the research says is for efficiency analysis, not detection; the add-backs the research treats as mandatory are not made; the ninety-day rule has no default.

### B.3 Rollforward flags

**What practitioners do.** Four readings of a reserve rollforward. Provision against usage: "it is reasonable to expect the ratio of bad debt expense to write-offs to be close to 1.0 over an extended period" and "a company might opportunistically reduce the allowance in a period of reduced earnings." (https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2009/sep/20091539/). Coverage against the gross base: "Bad debt reserves decline relative to gross receivables: Potential misclassification of receivables or significant write-offs of receivables; potential understatement of reserves." (https://doi.org/10.19030/jbcs.v13i4.10032). Prior-period estimate revisions: at Ford, "$2.7 billion of the 2024 expense, and $2.6 billion in 2023, came from raising estimates on vehicles sold in earlier periods, a sign that past reserves had been too low" (https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/accounting-investment-banking/reserves-and-allowances-bad-debt-returns-warranties). Releases: "When all reserve accruals are moving in the wrong direction (i.e., declining), head for the hills!" (https://linhth.com/2021/02/12/financial-shenanigans-fourth-edition-howard-schilit/) and "Creating acquisition-related reserves and releasing them into income in a later period" (https://www.scribd.com/document/465460323/Financial-Shenanigans). Goodwill: SEC staff expect, for a unit at risk, "The percentage by which fair value exceeded carrying value as of the date of the most recent test; The amount of goodwill allocated to the reporting unit; A description of the methods and key assumptions" (https://www.sec.gov/corpfin/cf-manual/topic-9), so the headroom percentage, and its absence, is the flag.

**What the repository does today.** Reserve ratios are balances over a base (`bad_debt_reserve_ratio`, `inventory_reserve_ratio`, `warranty_reserve_ratio` in the trend table, `src/trends.py` `RATIOS`); area 3 asks for *allowance, inventory reserve and warranty reserve ratios thinning* and *goodwill headroom* (`accounting-analyst.md:75-79`) with no field for provision, usage, revision or headroom. ### B.4 Release-versus-report and little-r numeric diffs

**What practitioners do.** The release is compared with the report line by line because "earnings releases are furnished to the SEC rather than filed as they are with the quarterly report, which means the company faces less liability for misstatement in an earnings release." (https://www.radicalcompliance.com/2019/02/25/discrepancies-corporate-disclosure); the window is "The average lag time between the filing of the 8-K and the filing of the 10-Q, was 3.4 days in 2018, and 3.7 days in 2017" (https://sec.gov/comments/s7-26-18/s72618-5177727-183527.pdf). The documented differences moved through tax and litigation lines while revenue stood, and every one found was adverse to the release: AmerisourceBergen's "The culprit was an increase in the company's litigation settlements line item, from $864.4 million to $914.4 million." Sunlight Financial's own words: "Subsequent to the Earnings Release but prior to the filing of its Quarterly Report on Form 10-Q" (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1762322/000176232223000034/sft2023331-ex991xdiscopslo.htm); Clover's correction came with "management determined that there was a material weakness" (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801170/000119312521102873/d118254d8ka.htm). Little r: "If the error is not material to previously-issued financial statements, but either correcting the error or leaving the error uncorrected would be material to the current period financial statements, a registrant must still correct the error, but is not precluded from doing so in the current period comparative financial statements by restating the prior period information and disclosing the error." and "we have observed that some materiality analyses appear to be biased toward supporting an outcome that an error is not material to previously-issued financial statements, resulting in 'little r' revision restatements." (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/munter-statement-assessing-materiality-030922). The database rule: "If there's a date, it's a Big R; if blank, it's a little r." (https://auditanalytics.help.ideagen.com/hc/en-gb/articles/35432233953554/).

### B.5 The contradiction standard

**What practitioners do.** Direction alone is a question. Three tests make a contradiction. Subtraction: "Software AG told CFRA that the contract only accounted for €13 million. Reversal: "a year after the company extended the payment terms for its customers, the year-over-year growth in DSO stopped." (same). ### B.10 Adjusted leverage: receivables sold and supplier finance in debt

**What practitioners do.** "We regard the securitization, sale, and factoring of trade receivables ... as being akin to secured financing. ## C. Practices the research found that this project should not adopt, and why

1. **Peer percentiles and industry-relative growth.** GMT: "We deem companies to raise red flags when their ratios exceed the 80th worst percentile relative to their peer group" (https://www.gmtresearch.com/en/accounting-ratio/percentile-scoring-against-a-relevant-peer-group/); CFRA: "Analysts are alerted when a covered company falls into the worst deciles on forensic accounting quality scores, prompting deeper review" (https://www.juliusbaer.com/fileadmin/legal/cfra-equity-research-methodology.pdf); Clapham's first check, "if the margins are higher than the peer group, then I ask myself, why is that?"; Brazel: "the difference between a company's revenue growth and the revenue growth of its industry has historically been the best fraud indicator" (https://www.newswise.com/pdf_docs/172467885077957_Brazel%20benchmarks%20and%20fraud%20risk%202024%20FINAL.pdf). Every one ranks a company against others. `CLAUDE.md` line 9, "no rank across companies"; `docs/HEDGE_FUND_ANALYSIS.md:457` and §5.5 (`:591`); the default in force at `docs/needs_judgment.md:217`. Own history is the comparison, as the research itself says accrual signals should be read.

2. **Contacting management, or not.** Schilit's CFRA: "we spoke to at least 75 percent of the companies and we would make at least three separate overtures to them." (https://irmagazine.com/reporting/shenanigans-qa). Nothing outside the filings enters a layer (`CLAUDE.md` line 11); the pipeline is on Chanos's side of this by construction and need not choose.

3. **Call transcripts, site visits, customs data, registries, former employees.** Block: "we'll typically print off three or four years worth of transcripts, and see how management's language has evolved." (https://moneyweek.com/investments/stocks-and-shares/carson-block-short-selling); Muddy Waters: "We sent investigators to 57 of Ensign's 379 facilities across eight states." (https://muddywatersresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/MW_ENSG_20260611.pdf); Hindenburg: "according to our review of more than 45,000 import/export transactions" (https://hindenburgresearch.com/smci/). Non-filing sources are excluded (`docs/HEDGE_FUND_ANALYSIS.md:459`, §5.5 `:593`; `docs/needs_judgment.md:218`). The independent-series test of §B.5 is run on the company's own filings only (its contract liabilities, its customers' concentration facts), never on a customer's filings (`:458`).

4. **Consensus and the beat rate as a measure.** FactSet's base rates (§B.9) rest on paid consensus; the catalogue already names *the free substitutes*, the company's own guidance and the surprise against its own history (`docs/HEDGE_FUND_ANALYSIS.md:282`); paid data stays out (§5.5 `:594`). The research reaches the same conclusion: a beat is the expected outcome and the informative measure is the surprise against the firm's own distribution, which is the §5.3 row (`:579`).

5. **Composite risk tiers.** GMT: "For a company to be classified as a low accounting risk it must not only populate all nine models, but not trigger any of them; meanwhile, very high-risk companies will generally trigger three models or more." (https://gmtresearch.com/en/accounting-ratio/accounting-risk-assessment); CFRA's Hazard List and Biggest Concerns. A count of triggered models is a composite. `CLAUDE.md` line 9; `docs/needs_judgment.md:216`; §5.5 (`:597`). The anomaly register with every item listed is the project's answer, and the research's own finding is that no named forensic shop publishes a cross-company composite.

6. **Paper thresholds as flags.** The −1.78 and −2.22 cutoffs, F above 1.00, 1.85 and 2.45, Montier 5–6, GMT's 20%. Beneish's own words make the cutoff a cost-ratio choice: "For investors, however, the relevant range is likely between 20:1 and 30:1." The default stands: printed as citations, no Python flag fires (`docs/needs_judgment.md:164`; `docs/HEDGE_FUND_ANALYSIS.md:466`, §5.5 `:596`). §B.7 adds the probability so a reader can apply a cost ratio without a flag.

7. **Letting the model do the arithmetic.** Daloopa's own measurement: "Numerical calculations: 52% accurate (this is why you never trust LLM math)" (https://daloopa.com/blog/analyst-best-practices/exploratory-financial-data-analysis-using-large-language-models). 8. **Machine-learned fraud models on raw items.** Bao et al.'s result softened in its own erratum: "RUSBoost no longer consistently outperforms the other models" (https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679x.12454); look-ahead bias inflates back-tests by "around 15 percent and can reach up to 30 percent" (https://doi.org/10.2308/tar-2023-0073). `CLAUDE.md` line 13: *the twelve companies test the pipeline, not the signal*. The accounting and financial analysts never see a price (`CLAUDE.md` line 11; `docs/HEDGE_FUND_ANALYSIS.md:460`; `docs/needs_judgment.md:219`); the market module labels items after the fact (`src/market_labels.py`).
