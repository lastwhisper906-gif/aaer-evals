# Hedge-fund-level analysis: methods, data fetched on demand, build order

Written 2026-10-28. It draws on six research sweeps (forensic accounting and short sellers; academic accounting-quality, distress and quality signals; fundamental long/short practice; credit and distress; public data sources; the semiconductor and hardware industry lens), a gap table against this repository, and a critic's review of both. Every correction the critic made is applied here. Claims the critic showed the sources do not support are dropped, and the critic's additions are included with their sources.

The owner's request, in English: the point is not to hold every piece of a company's information. It is to build a pipeline that fetches the information when it is needed, and then to do the accounting and financial analysis well. Find out in detail what analysis hedge funds do, and build a tool that analyses each company at the same level.

This document is a specification. It changes no rule, threshold, prompt or formula. Anything it implies for `rules/`, the tag-continuity map, an agent prompt or a calculator formula lands through its own pull request, the second lens (`docs/HOW_WE_WORK.md` §1, principle 13) and the next rules version.

How to read it:

- §1 is the one-page picture of how practitioners work.
- §2 is the method catalogue, one table per frame. §2.11 holds the published formulas.
- §3 lists the data the pipeline must fetch on demand, by source.
- §4 lists where these methods collide with this repository's rules, with the default for each.
- §5 is the build order: every item has a judge and says where its expected values come from.
- §6 lists every URL used.

Conventions:

- **Status here** is `covered`, `partly` or `missing`, with the file and line found on 2026-10-28. Line numbers drift, so grep for the name.
- **Priority** is high, medium or low.
- **Python or model**: "Python" means a number Python computes into `calculator.json`. "Model" means a reader or analyst reads filing text and quotes it verbatim. "Both" means Python computes and the model reads the note that explains it.
- **Layer**: *filings* means readers, and the accounting and financial analysts through `calculator_filings_only.json`. *valuation* means only the valuation analyst's priced calculator. *market* means the market module only.
- **Attribution**: a fund is named as a user only where its own publication shows the method. Short sellers' claims are allegations, and their outcomes are given where known.
- **Evidence**: published returns are upper bounds. Across 97 predictors, McLean and Pontiff find returns 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication.

## 1. What hedge funds do, in one page

**The accounting frame: can these numbers be trusted?** Practitioners start in the footnotes. In 2003 Jim Chanos told an SEC roundtable that Kynikos read Enron's footnotes "over and over again". He found a "cryptic disclosure regarding various related party transactions" and set Enron's pre-tax return on capital, "a paltry 7 percent", against a cost of capital "probably closer to 9 percent". The work has four layers.

1. **Ratio screens.** These measure the distance between earnings and cash, and between the balance sheet and sales: accruals, days of receivables, inventory and payables, soft assets, the depreciation rate and deferred revenue. Forensic research firms sell this work:
   - Behind the Numbers tracks days of receivables quarter by quarter.
   - CFRA rebuilds cash collected from customers and cash paid to suppliers.
   - GMT Research scores every line against industry peers and flags operating cash flow below 20% of adjusted cash earnings for two years running.
2. **Models trained on caught manipulators.** These are the Beneish M-score (1999) and the Dechow, Ge, Larson and Sloan F-score (2011). The F-score was trained on SEC Accounting and Auditing Enforcement Releases, so it is the model closest to this repository's target.
3. **Reading for the technique.** Schilit's taxonomy has four families:
   - earnings manipulation: revenue recorded too soon or bogus, one-time gains, expenses shifted between periods, reserve games;
   - cash flow: financing shown as operating, operating outflows routed through investing, unsustainable timing;
   - key metrics: misleading metrics; balance-sheet metrics distorted to hide deterioration;
   - acquisition accounting.
   SEC staff ask the same questions in comment letters: inventory days to Xilinx and Texas Instruments, payables days to Procter & Gamble and Masco, non-GAAP adjustments under the staff's interpretations.
4. **Outside records.** Short sellers test the filings against what the company does not control:
   - subsidiaries' statutory accounts (Muddy Waters on Sino-Forest and Orient Paper; Blue Orca on PDD);
   - corporate registries (Hindenburg on Adani);
   - affiliates' own reports (Gotham City on Grifols);
   - whether customers are independent (Grizzly on ESS Tech);
   - interest earned on reported cash (Muddy Waters on NMC Health and Eurofins);
   - auditor capacity (Hindenburg).

   Their record is mixed. Of the registry cases, only Sino-Forest is a clear win. Orient Paper, PDD and Monolithic Power are contested, and Grifols denied Gotham City's claims.

**The financial frame: how healthy is the company, and how does it make money?** Fundamental long/short analysts normalise first and decompose second.

- **Normalise.** Separate one-time from recurring items (Damodaran's four classes). Audit the non-GAAP reconciliation against the SEC staff's interpretations. Treat share-based compensation as a cost and define free cash flow several ways. Capitalise leases and R&D.
- **Decompose returns.** Return on invested capital on an adjusted base, incremental return on capital, DuPont and leverage-form ROE, gross profitability, segments, and price-volume-mix and margin bridges.
- **Judge capital allocation.** Buybacks against share-based compensation, acquisitions counted as reinvestment, a deal ledger, goodwill headroom, the metrics management is paid on, and insider trades.
- **Check delivery.** Compare each guided range with the first-reported actual.

Credit and distressed investors work from rating-agency method:

- S&P's liquidity sources and uses, and the maturity wall;
- covenant headroom read from the credit agreement, and add-back inflation in covenant EBITDA;
- adjusted debt and FFO to debt, interest coverage and the zombie test;
- going-concern language, late-filing notices and 8-K distress items;
- off-balance-sheet and working-capital financing, and pensions;
- a recovery waterfall, and a watch for liability-management exchanges.

**Valuation and positioning context.** The working tools are a discounted cash flow that keeps growth equal to reinvestment times the return on new capital, and the reverse DCF that reads the expectations in the price. Relative valuation, sum-of-the-parts and scenario-weighted values are standard alongside them. Positioning comes from:

- free sources: short interest, 13F holdings, 13D/13G filings and lock-up expiries;
- paid sources: securities-lending borrow cost, bond and CDS prices, and options skew.

**What the evidence supports.** These signals are reasons to read further, not trading rules. Two results are the strongest guide to where misstatement shows up:

- Dechow et al. (2011) find misstated revenue in 54.0% of 676 AAER firms.
- Raw accruals and the signed Dechow-Dichev residual detect AAER misstatements better than Jones-type discretionary accruals.

Accrual-anomaly returns have decayed since publication, and every published alpha below is an upper bound.

**How this maps onto this repository.**

| Frame | Who covers it here |
|---|---|
| accounting frame | the accounting analyst's seven areas and its industry lens |
| financial frame | the financial analyst |
| valuation and price-based positioning | the valuation analyst and the market module |

The pipeline already holds companyfacts, the submissions index with 8-K item codes, every numeric fact and TextBlock in the XBRL instance, MD&A, the controls sections, the risk-factor diff, Exhibit 21, Exhibit 10 (on trigger), the 8-K 2.02 release and four verbatim 8-K bodies, short interest and prices. What separates it from hedge-fund practice:

- rollforwards and splits of the balances it already reads (warranty, allowance, contract liabilities, inventory, supplier finance);
- multi-year histories of returns on capital;
- the earnings-release reconciliation parsed into rows;
- segments;
- liquidity and debt structure;
- filings it does not fetch yet: Form 4, DEF 14A, comment letters, NT filings and the other 8-K bodies;
- an on-demand fetch layer, instead of per-fixture batches.

### 2.11 Published formulas, as the papers give them

Coefficients and thresholds are copied from the papers named. A threshold is printed beside a field as a citation; it decides no flag until the owner sets thresholds in a rules version (§4).

- **Beneish M-score (1999).** M = −4.840 + 0.920 DSRI + 0.528 GMI + 0.404 AQI + 0.892 SGI + 0.115 DEPI − 0.172 SGAI + 4.679 TATA − 0.327 LVGI (unweighted probit); probability = Φ(M).
  - DSRI = (AR/Sales)_t ÷ (AR/Sales)_t−1.
  - GMI = gross margin_t−1 ÷ gross margin_t.
  - AQI = [1 − (CA + PPE)/TA]_t ÷ [same]_t−1.
  - SGI = Sales_t ÷ Sales_t−1.
  - DEPI = [Dep/(Dep + PPE)]_t−1 ÷ [same]_t, with Dep = depreciation less amortisation.
  - SGAI = (SGA/Sales)_t ÷ (SGA/Sales)_t−1.
  - LVGI = [(LTD + CL)/TA]_t ÷ [same]_t−1.
  - TATA in the paper = [(ΔCA − Δcash) − (ΔCL − Δcurrent maturities of LTD − Δincome tax payable) − D&A] ÷ TA_t. Many implementations use (income from continuing operations − CFO) ÷ TA instead; keep the two as separately named fields.
  - Cutoffs in the paper: M > −1.78 (probability > 0.0376) at 20:1 or 30:1 error-cost ratios, and M > −1.49 at 10:1. The −2.22 seen in secondary sources is not in the 1999 text.
  - Financial firms are excluded from the sample.
- **Dechow, Ge, Larson and Sloan F-score, Model 1 (2011).** Predicted value = −7.893 + 0.790 rsst_acc + 2.518 ch_rec + 1.191 ch_inv + 1.979 soft_assets + 0.171 ch_cs − 0.932 ch_roa + 1.029 issue. Probability = e^PV ÷ (1 + e^PV), and F = probability ÷ 0.0037 (494 ÷ 133,461).
  - F > 1 is "above normal risk" and F > 2.45 is "high risk".
  - The paper's worked example: Enron 2000 gives PV = −4.575, probability = 0.01020, F = 2.76.
- **Sloan balance-sheet accruals.** [(ΔCA − Δcash) − (ΔCL − ΔSTD − ΔTP) − Dep] ÷ average TA.
  **Hribar and Collins cash-flow accruals.** [income before extraordinary items − (CFO − CFO of discontinued operations)] ÷ lagged or average TA.
  **SEC Accounting Quality Model.** Total accruals = income before extraordinary items − free cash flow.
- **RSST.** TACC = ΔWC + ΔNCO + ΔFIN over average TA (definitions in §2.2).
  **Percent accruals.** (NI − CFO) ÷ abs(NI).
  **Net operating assets.** NOA = [(TA − cash and short-term investments) − (TA − short-term debt − long-term debt − minority interest − preferred − common equity)] ÷ TA_t−1.
- **Piotroski's nine signals** (each 1 if good):
  1. ROA > 0, with ROA = income before extraordinary items ÷ opening TA.
  2. CFO ÷ opening TA > 0.
  3. ΔROA > 0.
  4. CFO > ROA.
  5. Long-term debt ÷ average TA fell.
  6. Current ratio rose.
  7. No common equity issued.
  8. Gross margin rose.
  9. Asset turnover rose.

  The paper applies them within the top book-to-market quintile.
- **Montier's six flags:**
  1. a growing gap between NI and CFO;
  2. rising DSO;
  3. rising days of inventory;
  4. rising other current assets ÷ revenue;
  5. falling depreciation ÷ gross PPE;
  6. high total asset growth (no threshold given).
- **Altman Z (1968).** Z = 1.2 X + 1.4 Y + 3.3 E + 0.6 M + 1.0 S, where:
  - X = working capital ÷ TA;
  - Y = retained earnings ÷ TA;
  - E = EBIT ÷ TA;
  - M = market value of equity ÷ book total liabilities;
  - S = sales ÷ TA.

  The best-fit cutoff in the original tests was 2.675, and Altman's lower grey-zone bound is 1.81.

  **Altman Z′ (book equity).** 0.717 X + 0.847 Y + 3.107 E + 0.420 B + 0.998 S, where B = book equity ÷ total liabilities; distress below 1.23, safe above 2.90.

  **Altman Z″.** 6.56 X + 3.26 Y + 6.72 E + 1.05 B; the emerging-market variant adds 3.25. The 1.10 and 2.60 zone cutoffs often quoted for Z″ are not in the source read, so they are not used.
- **Ohlson O-score.** O = −1.32 − 0.407 SIZE + 6.03 TLTA − 1.43 WCTA + 0.0757 CLCA − 1.72 OENEG − 2.37 NITA − 1.83 FUTL + 0.285 INTWO − 0.521 CHIN. Probability = e^O ÷ (1 + e^O).
  - SIZE = log(TA ÷ GNP price-level index, 1968 = 100).
  - TLTA = total liabilities ÷ TA.
  - WCTA = working capital ÷ TA.
  - CLCA = current liabilities ÷ current assets.
  - OENEG = 1 if liabilities exceed assets.
  - NITA = net income ÷ TA.
  - FUTL = funds from operations ÷ total liabilities.
  - INTWO = 1 if net income was negative in the last two years.
  - CHIN = (NI_t − NI_t−1) ÷ (abs(NI_t) + abs(NI_t−1)).
- **Campbell, Hilscher and Szilagyi (12-month model, as reproduced in the thesis).** LPFD = −20.26 NIMTAAVG + 1.42 TLMTA − 7.13 EXRETAVG + 1.41 SIGMA − 0.045 RSIZE − 2.13 CASHMTA + 0.075 MB − 0.058 PRICE − 9.16, and PFD = 1 ÷ (1 + e^−LPFD).
  - The NIMTAAVG weights are 0.5333, 0.2666, 0.1333 and 0.0666.
  - Accounting data are lagged two months.
- **Naive distance to default (Bharath and Shumway).**
  - E = market cap; F = debt in current liabilities + half of long-term debt.
  - σ_V = E/(E + F) σ_E + F/(E + F)(0.05 + 0.25 σ_E); μ = the prior-year stock return; T = 1.
  - DD = [ln((E + F)/F) + (μ − 0.5 σ_V²)T] ÷ (σ_V √T); PD = N(−DD).
- **Mohanram's eight signals**, each against the median of low book-to-market firms in the same 2-digit SIC and year:
  1. ROA above the median;
  2. cash-flow ROA above the median;
  3. CFO above net income;
  4. variance of ROA over five years below the median;
  5. variance of sales growth below the median;
  6. R&D ÷ opening assets above the median;
  7. capex ÷ opening assets above the median;
  8. advertising ÷ opening assets above the median.
- **Dechow-Dichev with the McNichols terms (Francis et al.).** TCA_t = φ0 + φ1 CFO_t−1 + φ2 CFO_t + φ3 CFO_t+1 + φ4 ΔRev_t + φ5 PPE_t + u_t, scaled by average assets.
  - It is estimated by industry-year with at least 20 firms.
  - Accrual quality = the standard deviation of the firm's residuals over t−4 to t.
- **Jones and modified Jones.** TA_t/A_t−1 = a1(1/A_t−1) + a2(ΔREV_t/A_t−1) + a3(PPE_t/A_t−1). The modified form uses (ΔREV − ΔREC) in the test year.
  **Performance-matched (Kothari et al.).** The firm's discretionary accruals minus those of the same-industry firm with the closest ROA.
- **Roychowdhury.**
  - CFO_t/A_t−1 = a0 + a1(1/A_t−1) + b1(S_t/A_t−1) + b2(ΔS_t/A_t−1).
  - PROD = COGS + ΔInventory, on S_t, ΔS_t and ΔS_t−1.
  - DISEXP = advertising + R&D + SG&A, on S_t−1.
  - All are estimated by industry-year, and the abnormal part is actual − fitted. The suspect bin is NI ÷ assets in [0, 0.005).
- **Novy-Marx gross profitability.** (REVT − COGS) ÷ AT.
- **QMJ components.**
  - Profitability: GPOA, ROE, ROA, CFOA, GMAR and accruals ACC = −(ΔWC − DP) ÷ AT.
  - Growth: five-year changes in the profitability measures.
  - Safety: low beta, low leverage, Ohlson O, Altman Z and low ROE volatility.

### 2.12 Evidence, briefly

- **Upper bounds.** McLean and Pontiff: 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication. Sloan's accrual hedge is "no longer reliably positive" in the US (Green, Hand and Soliman 2011). Lazy Prices' 188 bp a month is the Risk Factors sort alone.
- **AAER base rates.** In Dechow et al. (2011), misstated revenue appears in 54.0% of 676 AAER firms, receivables in 19.1%, capitalised costs in 27.2%, inventory in 13.2%, reserves in 5.9% and the allowance in 4.3%. These say where to look first.
- **The Beneish M-score** works mainly through its ability to forecast changes in accruals, and most clearly among low-accrual stocks (Beneish, Lee and Nichols 2013).
- **Discretionary accruals** from Jones-type models are weaker than raw accruals at detecting AAERs, and performance matching adds estimation error (Dechow et al. 2011).
- **Short-seller cases are allegations.** Of the registry cases, only Sino-Forest is a clear win. Orient Paper, PDD and Monolithic Power are contested, and Grifols denied Gotham City's claims. Score registry-gap signals against their own outcomes.
- **This repository's own results.** The twelve companies test the pipeline, not the signal. None of the evidence above is a claim about this repository's results.

## 3. The data the pipeline must fetch on demand

### 3.1 Design

The owner's point is that the repository should not hold everything. The design follows from it:

- **One company at a time.** A run asks for one company and fetches only what its methods need. The per-company chain:
  1. ticker to CIK;
  2. the submissions index (one call);
  3. companyfacts (one call, about 3.8 MB for Apple);
  4. `index.json` for each needed accession;
  5. the documents themselves;
  6. efts for phrase searches;
  7. other sources by need.
- **Cache keyed by accession, sha256 per file.** An accession never changes once disseminated; an amendment is a new accession. The cache is local and disposable, and a corrupted file is refused, not re-read silently. What a run's agents saw is still committed in the run directory, because a published prediction must be reproducible from its published inputs alone. The cache is speed, the run directory is the record, and the committed fixtures stay the tests' source.
- **Every read passes `src/cutoff_guard.py`.** The cutoff key is each document's own `filingDate` from the submissions index, never a date recomputed from the acceptance time. Under Reg S-T 232.13, a filing accepted after 5:30 pm Eastern counts as filed the next business day. The exceptions run to 10 pm the same day: Forms 3, 4 and 5, Form 144, Schedule 14N and Schedules 13D/13G, and Rule 462(b) registration statements. Apple's Form 4 0001140361-26-040540 was accepted at 18:32:20 Eastern (22:32:20Z, which confirms `acceptanceDateTime` is UTC), and its `filingDate` is the same day.
- **SEC fair access.** At most 10 requests a second across SEC hosts, gzip accepted, and a declared User-Agent of the form "Company Name contact@domain". The contact comes from one environment variable the owner sets. It is never filled from the session's identity: a research session once sent a personal address as its User-Agent while probing hosts. No API key is needed, and CORS is not supported.
- **The bulk files are not needed per company.** `companyfacts.zip` was 1.41 GB and `submissions.zip` 1.57 GB on 2026-10-28, each rebuilt nightly. They become useful only for peer cross-sections, which are deferred (§4).
- **A changed response shape is a refusal, not silence.** This matters most for efts, which the SEC does not document as an API.

## 5. Build order

Every item is one pull request built with the `build-item` skill, briefed with `dispatch`, and entered in `docs/next_cycle_tasks.md` in that file's row shape (`title · builds · judge · expected value from · PR`) when it is launched.

- A change to a calculator formula, an agent prompt or `rules/` takes the second lens.
- A new tag-continuity pair is a rules change and applies from the next rules version.
- Each item is at most one day of infrastructure.
- Expected values are written into `tests/fixtures/{ticker}/expected_values.json` with a `from` note naming the source. They are read from the filing, the companyfacts record through `tests/companyfacts_source.py` (which imports nothing from `src/`), the paper's own worked example, or a value the test plants. They never come from a run of the code being judged.

Judges are written `.venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/<file> -q`. Every item also passes `make check`.

### 5.5 Needs judgment

These have no judge today, or depend on an owner question that §4 settles by default. They are not tasks and are not launched:

- peer-dependent methods (Jones-family regressions, Dechow-Dichev, Roychowdhury, Mohanram, unexplained audit fees, Novy-Marx ranks, comparables);
- other companies' data (hyperscaler capex, customer-supplier links);
- non-filing sources (registries, GLEIF, court records, rating releases, transcripts);
- paid data (consensus, TRACE feeds, CDS, borrow, options, alternative data);
- financial firms;
- turning paper thresholds into flags;
- composites as analyst inputs;
- the Loughran-McDonald licence.
